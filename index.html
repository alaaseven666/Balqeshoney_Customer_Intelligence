Patterns

What the segments are telling us

The five patterns that show up consistently across gender, geography, order value, and repeat-purchase behavior.

01

Vitality blends split sharply by gender

Men's Vitality Blend makes up 6.5% of male-inferred purchases vs 1.6% of female-inferred — the widest gender gap in the catalog. The Women's Blend shows the same pattern in reverse.

02

Demand is concentrated in four metro areas

Cairo, New Cairo, Alexandria and Giza together account for the large majority of orders — but as the delivery data shows, they don't perform equally.

03

Repeat buyers skew wellness, not honey

Customers with 2+ orders put 8.7% of their purchases into Men's Vitality Blend vs 5.0% for one-time buyers, and 5.5% into Propolis vs 2.9%.

04

Vitality blends are an add-on, not an entry point

Men's Vitality Blend has zero presence in the lowest order-value tier — nobody's first purchase is this product, it rides along with a bigger basket.

05

Cash on Delivery is nearly universal

94% of orders are COD. That's exactly why delivery outcome matters as much as the purchase itself — an order that isn't delivered is revenue that never actually lands.

Recommendations

What this means for marketing

Six concrete, data-backed moves — ranked by how directly they turn existing demand into delivered revenue.

!

Fix Giza's COD reliability before scaling Men's Vitality ads there

Giza has the strongest purchase intent for Men's Vitality Blend (23% of male orders) but only 20% of those actually get delivered. A confirmation call, deposit, or prepay-only rule for this SKU in this city would convert existing demand into real revenue — no new customers needed.

1

Invest confidently in Cairo, New Cairo & Alexandria

These three combine solid order volume with 77–85% delivery success. They're the safest geographies to put acquisition budget behind.

2

Lead retention campaigns with wellness, not plain honey

Repeat customers already over-index on Propolis and vitality blends. Win-back and loyalty messaging should feature these, not the classic honey jars.

3

Treat Propolis and Ginseng as the safest revenue in the catalog

At 97% and 95.7% delivery success, these categories convert almost every sale into cash in hand. They deserve premium placement, not just niche shelf space.

4

Test expanding the Women's Wellness line

Under 1% of revenue today, but delivery success is solid (85.7%) and it's the only line female-inferred customers show a real product-specific preference for. Looks underbuilt, not underwanted.

5

Add a verification step to low-value COD orders generally

The Mid-Low value tier has the weakest delivery success of any tier (72.8%) — consistent with low-commitment impulse orders being the easiest to refuse at the door.
